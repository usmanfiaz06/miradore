To the team at Marsal,

Further to our call last week, on 22 August, where we presented the brand evolution - we are glad the direction landed with you. This proposal follows that conversation, and, as you asked, it keeps the work to the brand evolution only.

To put the thinking on record: Marsal does not need a new logo. The one you have is doing its job, and there is real equity in it already sitting on your paperwork, your vehicles and your invoices. Redrawing it would be an expensive way to solve a problem you do not have.

What you do need is for that logo to work harder in the places it currently struggles. It has no short form, so there is nothing clean to put in a profile picture or an app icon. And when the Arabic and the English sit together, the signature runs wide and low - which is why it keeps getting shrunk into illegibility on cards, on nav bars, and on the side of a truck.

So the work here is evolution, not replacement. We pull a proper logomark out of the identity you already own. We consolidate the bilingual signature so Arabic and Latin hold equal weight in a footprint that fits. We settle the colour and the type. Then we write it all down in a guide short enough that people will actually open it.

That is why there is no stationery, no social templates and no website in what follows. Not because you will not need them, but because every one of them is cheaper and better once the mark is settled. When you are ready, we will price that against a mark whose behaviour we already know.

As you saw on the call, we did not bring a spread of logo options to pick from. We did the thinking, committed to one direction, and showed it applied to real things - a card, a truck panel, a profile picture - so you were judging a decision rather than a shape on a white page. That approach is what the rest of this schedule is built on.

With the direction approved, what remains is the refinement, the colour and type, and the guide that documents it. Everything lands with you on Thursday 27 August.

If any part of this reads as more, or less, than what you had in mind, tell us and we will reshape it. We would much rather adjust the scope now than hand you something you have to explain internally.

Warm regards,

Usman Fiaz

Where the identity
stands today

An honest read of the current logo, and the three things holding it back.

This is not a criticism of the mark. It is a list of the jobs it is being asked to do that it was never drawn to do. All three are fixable without losing anything you have already built.

01

There is no short form

Every strong logistics brand carries two assets: a full signature for documents and letterhead, and a compact mark for everything else. Marsal has only the first. A profile picture becomes a squeezed wordmark, an app icon is not possible, and there is nothing to stitch onto a uniform, cut into vinyl for a vehicle door, or stamp onto a seal. Below roughly 25 mm wide, it simply stops reading.

02

The bilingual lockup runs too wide

Placed side by side, the Arabic and English signature becomes long and shallow. In a business card corner, a website navigation bar or a truck panel, the only way to fit it is to scale it down until neither language is legible. The two scripts also carry different optical weights, so one visually dominates the other depending on the size.

03

There is no system to work from

Without an agreed set of rules for the mark, colour, type and spacing, every new card, post or document is a fresh set of decisions - made by whoever happens to be making it. It is slower, it costs more over a year, and the drift shows, especially to the enterprise clients and government tenders where visual consistency is read as operational discipline.

What we are proposing

Three moves. Everything else in this document follows from them.

A
The mark

A logomark drawn out of the logo you already own

We do not start with a blank page. We take the existing Marsal logo apart, find the element carrying the most recognition, and develop it into a mark that stands alone. It has to survive four tests before we present it: legible at 16 pixels, readable in a single colour, cuttable in vinyl without detail loss, and distinct from the other logistics marks operating in the Kingdom.

One direction, developed properly - not a spread of options to choose between. We show it applied to real things, so you are judging a decision rather than a shape on a white page.

03

Scope of work,
phase by phase

Four phases. The identity core, and the guide that documents it.

Phase 01

Read and audit

  • Review of the existing logo files, and how the mark is being applied today
  • Benchmark of eight to ten logistics identities operating in KSA and the wider Gulf
  • A working session with your team - what the brand needs to carry, and where it keeps failing
  • A one-page direction note, agreed before any design begins
Phase 02

Logomark and bilingual signature

  • The logomark, developed from the existing Marsal logo and presented in application
  • Full construction: proportion grid, clear space, minimum sizes
  • Consolidated bilingual lockups - horizontal, stacked and mark-only
  • One-colour, reversed, mono and vinyl-cut versions for production
  • Icon renderings at 16, 32, 64, 128 and 512 px, plus favicon and app-icon files
  • Master artwork delivered as AI, EPS, SVG, PDF and PNG
Phase 03

Colour palette and typography

  • Primary and secondary palette, built to hold up in full colour and in one
  • A reference for every medium - CMYK and Pantone for print, RGB and HEX for screen, vinyl and paint codes for the fleet
  • Contrast checked on every colour pairing the brand will actually use
  • A matched Arabic and Latin typeface pairing, with weights, hierarchy and usage rules
Phase 04

Brand guide and handover

  • The brand guide as a PDF, for screen and print: the mark and its construction, clear space and minimum sizes, the bilingual lockups and when to use each, colour, typography, and correct against incorrect usage
  • Organised master file library, named and structured so files can be found without us
  • A handover walkthrough with your team
  • 14 days of post-handover support for file and format questions

What we need
from Marsal

Four things. Nothing that should take more than an hour to gather.

  • One named approver for the final review. This schedule does not survive design by committee, and it is the single biggest risk to the date.
  • Your Arabic legal name exactly as registered, letter for letter. The bilingual signature is drawn on it, and a correction later means redrawing it.
  • Any colour you are already committed to - a paint code on the fleet, a value sitting in the current logo files - so the palette is built around it rather than against it.
  • Two or three photographs showing where the mark appears today - a vehicle, a sign, a document - so we can see what it is actually up against.
How it
runs

You hear from us at two points, not ten. The first was the direction presentation on 22 August, which you have approved. The second is the final review, where you see the mark, the lockups, colour and type together before the guide is produced. Between those points we work, and we do not send fragments for reaction. It keeps the decisions clean, and it keeps the date.
